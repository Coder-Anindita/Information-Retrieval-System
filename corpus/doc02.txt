An inverted index is the central data structure behind almost every search engine. It maps each term in the vocabulary to the list of documents in which that term appears. Building the index involves collecting documents, tokenizing the text, normalizing the tokens and then recording each term with its document identifiers. Because the index is organized by term rather than by document, a query can be answered by looking up only the relevant terms. This makes search extremely fast even for collections containing millions of documents. The index can also store term frequencies and positions for ranking and phrase queries.
