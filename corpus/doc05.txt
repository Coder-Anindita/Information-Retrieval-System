Boolean retrieval is one of the oldest and simplest models of information retrieval. In this model, queries are written using operators like AND, OR and NOT to combine terms precisely. An AND query returns documents containing all the terms, an OR query returns documents containing any of the terms, and NOT excludes documents containing a term. The results are computed by intersecting, merging or subtracting posting lists from the inverted index. Boolean retrieval gives users exact control, which is why it is still popular with lawyers and librarians. Its main weakness is that it does not rank documents by relevance.
