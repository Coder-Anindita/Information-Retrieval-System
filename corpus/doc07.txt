Search engines like Google, Bing and DuckDuckGo help billions of people find information on the web every day. They work in three main stages: crawling, indexing and ranking. Crawlers discover and download web pages, the indexer builds a huge inverted index from the page content, and the ranking system orders results for every query. Modern search engines use hundreds of signals for ranking, including links, freshness and user location. They also offer features like spelling correction, query suggestions and instant answers. Handling the scale of the web requires thousands of servers working together.
