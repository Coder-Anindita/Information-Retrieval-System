Precision and recall are two key measures used to evaluate the quality of a retrieval system. Precision is the fraction of retrieved documents that are actually relevant to the query. Recall is the fraction of all relevant documents in the collection that were successfully retrieved. There is usually a trade-off between them, since returning more documents increases recall but lowers precision. The F-measure combines precision and recall into a single number using their harmonic mean. Other evaluation metrics include mean average precision and normalized discounted cumulative gain, which consider the ranking order.
